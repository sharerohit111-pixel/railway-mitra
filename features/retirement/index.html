Understand the two pension options

Before using the calculator, learn how NPS and UPS work. These are different retirement benefit structures; the better fit depends on eligibility, service, corpus and personal priorities.

NPS – National Pension System

NPS is a defined-contribution, market-linked retirement system. Contributions are invested in pension funds and the retirement corpus depends on contributions, investment choices, returns and charges.

How does it work?

  • For Central Government employees, employee contribution is generally 10% of Basic Pay + DA and Government contribution is 14%.
  • Money is invested through a registered Pension Fund Manager according to the selected investment pattern.
  • At exit/retirement, the accumulated corpus is used for lump-sum withdrawal and annuity/pension as per applicable exit rules.

In simple terms: the final benefit is linked to the fund accumulated and the annuity selected; a fixed monthly pension is not assured by NPS itself.

UPS – Unified Pension Scheme

UPS is an assured-payout option under NPS for eligible Central Government employees, effective from 1 April 2025. It combines a defined contribution structure with a conditional, service-linked assured payout.

How does it work?

  • Employee contributes 10% of Basic Pay + DA; Government contributes a matching 10% to the individual corpus and an additional 8.5% to the pool corpus.
  • Full assured payout is generally 50% of the average Basic Pay of the last 12 months after 25 years of qualifying service; proportionate payout applies for 10–25 years.
  • Minimum guaranteed payout is ₹10,000 per month after at least 10 years of qualifying service, subject to scheme conditions.
  • Dearness Relief applies to admissible payout; eligible spouse family payout is 60% of the subscriber's admissible payout.

In simple terms: UPS offers a more predictable, service-linked payout, but the payable amount can be reduced in specified corpus-shortfall or final-withdrawal situations.
