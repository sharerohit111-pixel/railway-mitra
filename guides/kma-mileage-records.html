Mileage and duty records

Kilometre Allowance: Keeping a Reliable Mileage Record

In Railway rules, Kilometrage Allowance is part of the running-allowance framework for eligible running staff. It should not be confused with a general travel or conveyance allowance for every employee. The employee category, type of duty, accepted kilometre record and rate in force all matter.

1. Understand who the rule covers

The Indian Railway Establishment Code, Chapter 15 describes running allowance and identifies running staff and kilometre-based payments. The Railway Servants (Running Allowance) Rules set out the framework for kilometrage and allowance in lieu of kilometrage. These provisions concern specified running staff; do not assume a track-maintenance or other non-running post qualifies for KMA merely because kilometres are travelled during work.

2. Separate running duty from non-running duty

The rules distinguish kilometrage allowance for qualifying running duties from allowance in lieu of kilometrage for specified non-running duties. The latter is not an automatic substitute for every day away from a train-running assignment; check the listed circumstances and current instructions. Record the duty type accurately so that the correct rule can be applied.

3. Keep the accepted duty and mileage evidence

  • Date and duty period.
  • Employee category and duty type.
  • Train, trip or other prescribed duty reference where applicable.
  • Authorised kilometre entry or accepted distance source.
  • Any required certificate, approval or correction reference.
  • Pay month in which the entry is claimed or credited.

Use the register or system prescribed by the administration. A consumer map route, personal odometer or informal distance estimate should not replace the accepted railway record unless the applicable procedure explicitly permits it.

4. Check the applicable rate and date

Rates are prescribed by the competent authority and may change. Check the current Railway Board instruction for the employee category and duty type, its effective date and any conditions. Do not use an old rate table or copy another employee's rate without confirming that the same rule applies.

5. Reconcile the month

  1. Group accepted entries by duty date and category.
  2. Compare the total with the authorised register or system.
  3. Apply the rate and method effective for that period.
  4. Check corrections, cancellations and any previous payment.
  5. Compare the result with the pay slip and list unresolved differences separately.
