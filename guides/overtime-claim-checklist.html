Overtime Claim: A Practical Verification Checklist

Overtime Allowance (OTA) is not simply payment for every hour spent beyond a personal estimate of normal duty. The applicable Railway rules, Hours of Employment Regulations, employee category, nature of work and accepted duty records determine whether overtime is payable and how it is assessed.

1. Read the governing rule first

The Indian Railway Establishment Code, Chapter 15 describes overtime and running allowances for Group C and D Railway servants. Rule 1502 describes overtime allowance as payment for actual time worked in excess of the hours of employment prescribed by law or rule. Rule 1503 refers to the Hours of Employment Regulations and the staff categories for which overtime is payable. Read the current applicable provisions and amendments for the employee concerned.

2. Distinguish rostered hours from actual hours

Keep the approved duty roster or schedule alongside the authorised attendance and actual duty record. Record the date, start and finish times, breaks and any shift crossing midnight. Do not double-count the same period across registers or assume that every extension of a shift automatically qualifies.

3. Confirm category and authorisation

Check whether the employee's category is covered by the relevant rule and whether the required approval, duty certificate or administrative record exists. Some provisions distinguish supervisory, management or confidential positions and particular work establishments. Do not apply another employee's eligibility or calculation to your own case.

4. Build a transparent worksheet

  1. Pay month and duty date.
  2. Employee category and applicable rule reference.
  3. Prescribed hours or rostered schedule.
  4. Actual start/end times and qualifying extra time.
  5. Breaks or intervals treated under the applicable regulation.
  6. Approval/certificate and source-register reference.
  7. Calculation method, amount claimed and amount already paid.

5. Reconcile with the pay slip

Compare the approved claim with the OTA entry in the relevant pay period. Check whether the payment appears later than the duty month, whether a correction was made and whether a returned claim cites a missing record. Keep the original record and document any correction instead of silently changing figures.

6. Ask a precise question

If the amount differs, state the month, duty date, hours recorded, rule or order used, amount claimed and amount credited. Ask the authorised personnel/accounts office to confirm the applicable rule and accepted calculation basis.

Official reference

Indian Railway Establishment Code – Chapter 15: Overtime and Running Allowances

Educational information only. This guide is not an overtime sanction or eligibility decision. Follow current Railway Board instructions, Hours of Employment Regulations, zonal/divisional procedures and the authorised office's directions.
